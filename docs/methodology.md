# Methodology

## Goal
Give early-stage founders a fast, transparent first screening of possible foreign markets. The score answers "Where should I look first?", not "Where will I succeed?".

## Data
All indicators come from the World Bank API (World Development Indicators, Worldwide Governance Indicators, Global Findex), years 2005-2024, 42 countries. The dashboard uses the newest year in which at least 20 countries can be scored for the chosen profile.

If a country has no value for a year, the last known value of the same country is carried forward. The allowed number of years differs per indicator (3 years for annual statistics, up to 6 for survey data such as account ownership, which is published every few years).

If a country still lacks an indicator, its score is computed from the criteria it has (weights renormalised) and the dashboard shows the share of weights that had data. Countries with less than 70 % coverage are left out of that ranking and listed.

Indicators marked optional in `src/config.py` are skipped if the World Bank does not deliver them. The profile weights are then renormalised over the remaining indicators.

## Scoring steps
1. **Cap outliers.** Values below the 5th or above the 95th percentile (across countries, same year) are set to those limits.
2. **Scale to 0-100.** The lowest capped value gets 0 points, the highest 100. Skewed indicators (population, researchers, secure servers) are compared on a log scale.
3. **Direction.** Most indicators are "higher is better". Inflation is scored by its distance from 2 %. For a *financial inclusion* fintech, a low account ownership is the opportunity, so that indicator is inverted for this focus.
4. **Weights.** Each industry focus has base weights (`INDUSTRIES` in `src/config.py`). They are multiplied by factors for the business model (B2B/B2C), the risk priority (balanced, growth, safety) and the budget (a small budget raises the weight of predictability: inflation and rule of law), then normalised to sum to 1.
5. **Manual factors (optional).** The user can scale any weight between 0 % and 200 % before normalising.
6. **Add up.** Score = sum of (points x weight).

## Explanations
The data briefing is generated by fixed rules from the numbers: strengths are criteria with at least 60 points (largest weighted contribution first), risks are criteria below 40 points (largest weighted shortfall first), and trends compare the current value with the value five years earlier.

## AI advisor
The advisor receives the startup profile, the weights and an evidence table for the top six countries (values, points, weights, trends). Its instructions require it to use only these numbers, to justify each recommendation with at least three data points, to name the main risk, to compare the top countries, and to say what the data cannot show. Text from the founder is passed in marked blocks and treated as data, not as instructions. The model can still make mistakes; important facts must be verified. An automatic check of every number in the output is on the roadmap.

## Known limits
- Scores are **relative** to the other countries in the data set, not absolute.
- National averages hide regional differences (cities vs. countryside).
- The score does not see competitors, taxes, licences, language, culture, distance or the product itself.
- World Bank data is published with delay; some indicators lag by one to two years.
- Weights are a judgement call, informed by what each industry needs. They are visible in the app and in `src/config.py` and can be challenged.
- Five industries have their own indicators (Fintech, SaaS, HealthTech, CleanTech/energy, E-commerce/consumer); "Other industry" uses only the six core criteria and says so to the AI adviser. 42 countries so far.
- Rich, small countries rank high in most profiles because most indicators reward wealth and institutions. Use the weight sliders and the AI adviser to challenge this.
