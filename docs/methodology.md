# Methodology

## Goal
Give early-stage founders a fast, transparent first screening of possible foreign markets. The score answers "Where should I look first?", not "Where will I succeed?".

## Data
All indicators come from the World Bank API (World Development Indicators and Worldwide Governance Indicators). Years 2005-2024 are downloaded; the dashboard uses the newest year in which at least 20 countries have all five indicators.

If a country has no value for a year, the last known value of the same country is carried forward for at most 3 years. Countries that still lack an indicator are left out of that year's ranking.

## Scoring steps
1. **Cap outliers.** Values below the 5th or above the 95th percentile (across countries, same year) are set to those limits.
2. **Scale to 0-100.** The lowest capped value gets 0 points, the highest 100.
3. **Inflation is special.** Both very high inflation and deflation make planning hard, so inflation is scored by its distance from 2 % (closer is better).
4. **Weight and add.** Each sub-score is multiplied by its weight (weights are normalised to sum to 1).

## Explanations
The text in the "Country analysis" section is generated by fixed rules from the numbers: it names the two strongest criteria, the weakest one, and flags criteria below 30 points. No claim is made that cannot be traced to a data point.

## Known limits
- Scores are **relative** to the other countries in the data set, not absolute.
- Equal treatment of all industries: a country's score does not yet depend on the founder's sector or target group.
- Country-level averages hide regional differences (e.g. cities vs. countryside).
- World Bank data is published with delay; some indicators lag by one to two years.
- Weights are a judgement call. Default weights favour growth (30 %); users can change them.
- Not included yet: market size, taxes, funding landscape, language, distance, regulation of specific industries.
